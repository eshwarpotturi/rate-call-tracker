What forecasters said about the Fed, and what came true

The tracker starts from a rate decision and asks who saw it coming. This page starts from the other end. It takes what banks, surveys and the Fed itself said about the fed funds rate from 2016 to 2026, turns each statement into a claim with a deadline, and checks it against what the Fed did. Every claim links to the report it came from.

    How it works

    Mine, check, benchmark: the same three steps on any research corpus

    1 · ExtractArticle in, claims out

    An LLM reads each report and returns every forecast as a structured claim: who, when, what level or move, by what deadline, and the exact phrase it came from. claims/extract.py runs this through LLM Foundry.

    2 · VerifyClaims against facts

    claims/verify.py checks each claim against the Fed's actual rate path with fixed rules, so the same data always gives the same verdict. Claims still open are marked untested, or needing revision when the facts since make them unreachable.

    3 · BenchmarkVerdicts into a record

    Verdicts roll up by house, by year and by horizon. Internally that rates analysts; externally it shows clients how much weight each source has earned.

    Verdicts

      Where it goes next

      • Run it on a research team's own notes, not press coverage, to rate its analysts the way McKinsey's energy outlooks were checked.
      • Re-run nightly: a claim flips to "needs revision" the day a data print or decision makes it unreachable, which is the moment to update a client note.
      • Same code for ECB, BoE and RBI rates, then GDP, inflation and earnings estimates: only the fact table changes.
      Limits

      Check before sending outside

      • Claims come from news coverage of each house's forecast, not the original research notes. A house's record here is only as complete as what the press reported.
      • The 2023 levels come from a Bloomberg table that gives single numbers; they are read as the top of the target range.
      • J.P. Morgan's "3.75% through September 2025" is read as the top of the 3.50%-3.75% range.
      • The Fed's dot-plot medians and the rate path were entered from the Fed's published tables; spot-check a few against the linked pages.
      • Open-claim checks assume Fed meetings on 28 October and 9 December 2026.
