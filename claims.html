How this works, and what to check before sending it outside
1 · ExtractArticle in, forecasts out

An LLM reads each report and lists every forecast: who, when, what, by when, and the exact phrase. claims/extract.py runs this through LLM Foundry.

2 · VerifyForecasts against facts

claims/verify.py checks each forecast against the Fed's rate path with fixed rules. The LLM never decides a verdict.

3 · BenchmarkVerdicts into a record

Verdicts roll up by house, year and horizon: a rating for analysts inside, a track record for clients outside.

  • Correct = rate in the same target range as called, by the deadline. Partly = one 25 bp move off, or right direction but wrong size, or the right move up to 60 days off. Wrong = anything further off. Needs revision = deadline ahead, but it now needs more moves than meetings left, or a reversal of the Fed's last move.
  • Forecasts come from news coverage of each house's view, not the original research notes. Run on a team's own notes, the same steps rate its analysts.
  • The 2023 levels come from a Bloomberg table of single numbers, read as the top of the target range. J.P. Morgan's "3.75% through September 2025" is read the same way.
  • The Fed's rate path and dot-plot medians were entered from the Fed's tables; spot-check a few against the linked pages. Open forecasts assume meetings on 28 October and 9 December 2026.
